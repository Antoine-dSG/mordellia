Insight record

Assessment after the opening synthesis

The Preface and full Introduction were read before choosing the exposition. Chapter I’s central mechanism is projection from an ordinary node and the resulting sextic with contact conic. The largest explanatory gains concern curve normalization, degree-two maps, divisor classes and rank loci. Introducing a universal K3 or moduli-space framework at the outset would conceal the calculations before explaining them.

Provisional audience for this treatment: a reader comfortable with homogeneous polynomials, linear algebra and basic projective geometry, willing to learn the needed curve-theoretic definitions locally. Adjunction, Riemann–Roch, the canonical-map theorem and Riemann–Hurwitz are explicitly invoked. The determinantal class formula is optional. This is an editorial choice for Jessop Chapter I, not a new audience requirement across Mordellia.

The bounded Mordellia review required no further user question: the selected book, chapter order, three layers and emphasis on explanatory structure were already explicit. A deeper classification project, a full scholarly transcription, and an exhaustive novelty investigation remain separate scopes.

Directions left open

  1. Compare the several double covers explicitly. For a chosen syzygetic example, relate the branch sextic’s hyperelliptic involution to the genus-one family on the quartic. The objects and dimensions differ; no identification is inferred from shared degree two.
  2. Track the two-torsion obstruction under degeneration. Even contact on a positive-genus normalization can define a nontrivial unramified cover, whereas genus zero forces splitting. A concrete family could show how Jessop’s cases meet. This is a proposed experiment using known theory, not a claim that the degeneration problem is open in the literature.
  3. Analyze the relation-line discriminant. The extra-node conditions are two binary quadratics. Computing their resultant and its degenerate strata for a six-plane family could organize repeated roots, collisions and worse singularities. The generic singularity calculation is established here; a complete stratification is not.

No new invariant, original theorem or exhaustive classification has been established. The searches were targeted at canonical maps, nodal sextics, symmetric determinants and Kummer surfaces. They support the mathematical dictionary, not an originality claim. Reader understanding and independent expert review have not been measured.
