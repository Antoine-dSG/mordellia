Edition and source

C. M. Jessop, Quartic Surfaces with Singular Points, Cambridge University Press, 1916. The title page names Jessop alone as author; the Preface is dated March 1916 and acknowledges H. F. Baker’s encouragement and criticism. The scanned copy has xxxv preliminary pages and 197 numbered pages, followed by printer matter. The PDF has 244 images, including covers and blanks.

Source: Internet Archive, quarticsurfacesw00jessiala. Retained source PDF. The Archive OCR was used to locate passages; formulas and published excerpts were checked against page images. OCR is not the Original layer.

Page map and scope

The Preface is PDF image 9 (unnumbered, conventionally p. v). The Introduction is pp. xi–xxxv, PDF images 15–39. Chapter I is pp. 1–23, PDF images 41–63: add 40 to its printed page number. The Contents and Addenda were also read. No Chapter I correction appears in the printed Addenda.

The opening synthesis used the full Introduction, whose later-chapter summaries provide context. The developed edition is limited to the Preface, selected Introduction passages relevant to Chapter I, and a treatment of Chapter I §§1–12. Later chapters have not been developed. See coverage.

Under the ordinary UK literary-work term, life plus seventy years ending at the calendar year’s end, the term for his text expired at the end of 2009. The reproduction uses the historical edition, not new commentary or typesetting from a recent publisher. These findings support use of the original text in the US and UK; other jurisdictions and unusual rights issues have not been exhaustively reviewed. Transcription release

Line wrapping, page headers, line-end hyphenation, spacing and quotation typography are normalized. Mathematical notation is rendered with KaTeX. Collation was performed by the authoring assistant against the retained scans. Independent scholarly collation is outstanding. Source errors are preserved, including the p. 20 determinant mismatch. Details and other qualifications are in the reading pages’ Edition notes.

Modern mathematical inputs

The reconstructions give their local arguments explicitly. Their principal external inputs are adjunction and the genus formula for nodal curves; Riemann–Roch and the canonical-map theorem; Riemann–Hurwitz; the expected-codimension determinantal formula; and the degree of the rank-two locus of symmetric matrices. They are not represented as newly proved theorems.

  • A. Buium, Algebraic Curves, Lecture 14: the hyperelliptic/canonical-map dichotomy.
  • Stacks Project, Algebraic Curves, especially Riemann–Hurwitz: the curve-theoretic framework.
  • William Fulton, Intersection Theory, second edition, §14.4: the Thom–Porteous formula, used conditionally on expected codimension for the degrees 6 and 18.
  • M. Helsø, Rational Quartic Symmetroids, §2: the symmetric determinantal description and generic degree-ten node locus. This paper’s rational-surface classification is not applied to ordinary nodal K3 surfaces.
  • I. Dolgachev, K3 Surfaces of Kummer Type in Characteristic Two, Introduction: the characteristic-not-two Kummer quotient and quartic model, cited only for that introductory background. This edition works over the complex numbers.

These references substantiate known structures. A bounded search did not establish priority for the precise pedagogical juxtaposition with Jessop. That absence is not evidence of originality.
