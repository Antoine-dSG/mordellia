## 6. Eight nodes

Let the surface be $u_2w^2+2u_3w+u_4=0$, with projection centre $[0:0:0:1]$. A quadric through that point has equation $2t_1w+t_2=0$. At another surface node, $u_2w+u_3=0$, so elimination gives

$$u_2t_2-2t_1u_3=0.$$

This plane quartic passes through the seven projected nodes and the six contact points of the sextic with $u_2=0$. Jessop uses a one-to-one correspondence between such quartics and quadrics through the eight surface nodes.

In the general case, these quadrics form a pencil. In the special case, they form a net: the eight nodes are the common intersection of three quadrics $A,B,C$, and the surface has equation

$$Q(A,B,C)=0,$$

with $Q$ quadratic. A quadric of the net meets the quartic in two space quartics, each a complete intersection of two quadrics. Their projections are cubics through the seven plane nodes.

Jessop calls the pencil case *asyzygetic* and the net case *syzygetic*. His footnote also represents the latter as the envelope of $\lambda^2D+\lambda E+F=0$, with $D,E,F$ quadrics through the eight nodes.

## The dianodal surface

For the seven-nodal family $F=Q(A,B,C)+\rho\Sigma$, differentiation gives

$$\partial_iF=Q_A\partial_iA+Q_B\partial_iB+Q_C\partial_iC+\rho\partial_i\Sigma.$$

At an additional node the four derivatives vanish. Jessop concludes that

$$\det\big(\nabla A,\nabla B,\nabla C,\nabla\Sigma\big)=0.$$

Thus an eighth node is constrained to a degree-six surface, the *dianodal surface*. The exception at the common base points of $A,B,C$ is explained in the edition notes.

Given eight asyzygetic nodes, two quadrics $A,B$ through them and an eight-nodal quartic $T$, the family is

$$\alpha A^2+\beta B^2+2\gamma AB+2\rho T=0.$$

## 7. Nine nodes

For a syzygetic quartic $Q(A,B,C)=0$, an additional node must lie where

$$\operatorname{rank}\begin{pmatrix}\nabla A\\\nabla B\\\nabla C\end{pmatrix}\le2.$$

Jessop identifies this degree-six space curve with the vertices of singular quadrics in the net $A+\lambda B+\mu C$. Thus a cone $K$ through the eight associated points has its vertex at the ninth node.

Projection from this vertex gives a plane sextic with eight nodes on the conic defined by $K$. The conic must be a component, leaving a quartic. Jessop obtains

$$A^2+\rho KB=0,$$

where $A$ passes through all nine nodes and $B$ through the eight associated points. He states that the family of nine-nodal syzygetic surfaces is three-dimensional.

For an asyzygetic eight-nodal surface $T$, the ninth node instead lies on

$$\operatorname{rank}\begin{pmatrix}\nabla A\\\nabla B\\\nabla T\end{pmatrix}\le2,$$

the degree-$18$ dianodal curve. Once it is chosen, Jessop gives a pencil $A^2+\rho P=0$, with $A$ the quadric through the nine nodes and $P$ a quartic having them. The rectangular double bars in Original mean that all maximal minors vanish.
