## 4. Six nodes on a conic

Let $c_2=0$ be a conic through six nodes of a sextic, and $c_3=0$ a cubic through those points. Jessop writes a sextic through their complete intersection as

$$c_2c_4+c_3c_3'=0.$$

The double-point conditions give the form

$$c_3^2+c_2c_3A+c_2^2B=0.$$

Completing the square gives $K_3^2-c_2^2V=0$, where $V$ is quadratic. Consequently the corresponding quartic

$$w^2V+2wK_3+c_2^2=0$$

has $w=0$ as a *trope*, a plane whose intersection with the surface is a conic counted twice. This follows Jessop’s use of the equations; the source’s implicit genericity is discussed separately.

## Seven nodes and paired points

Let $D_1,\ldots,D_7$ be the nodes. Jessop distinguishes whether two further points $P,P'$ on the sextic can lie on a pencil of cubics through all seven nodes.

Suppose they can, and choose two cubics $c_3,c_3'$ in that pencil. Since the sextic contains their complete intersection, it can be written

$$c_3\Gamma_3+c_3'\Gamma_3'=0.$$

Besides the seven nodes and $P,P'$, the cubic $c_3$ meets the sextic at two residual points $Q,Q'$. The equation forces $\Gamma_3'$ through these points and the seven nodes. Thus $Q,Q'$ also lie on a pencil of cubics through the nodes. Varying $c_3$ gives the paired-point involution. When $Q=P$, its partner $Q'$ equals $P'$, so a cubic tangent once is tangent twice.

The four cubics $c_3,c_3',\Gamma_3,\Gamma_3'$ belong to the three-dimensional vector space of cubics through the nodes. Their linear dependence expresses the sextic as

$$Q(\phi,\psi,\chi)=0,$$

where $\phi,\psi,\chi$ form a basis of that vector space and $Q$ is a quadratic form.

## Eight nodes

In the preceding source calculation, $f=0$ is the sextic and $c_9=0$ the degree-nine Jacobian curve associated with the pencil of cubics through the eight nodes. Their intersections away from the nodes number

$$9\cdot6-8\cdot6=6.$$

These are the tangencies, giving six tangent cubics through the eight nodes. The construction of $c_9$ is outside the selected excerpt; it is described in Reinterpreted before the alternative count.

If the sextic has nine nodes, the pencil through eight of them has four tangent cubics. For a sextic with ten nodes, the corresponding number is two.

## 5. Ten nodes and a lift to a quadric

Take a quadric projecting from $O$ with the given contact conic as branch conic. Intersect it with the cone over the ten-nodal sextic. Jessop counts $26$ actual double points on the degree-$12$ intersection curve: two over each of the ten nodes and one over each of the six contacts. He also counts $30$ apparent double points in a generic plane projection, invoking Salmon, *Geometry of Three Dimensions*, fifth edition, vol. I, p. 356.

The total $56$ exceeds $55$, the maximum genus of an irreducible plane curve of degree $12$. Jessop concludes that the intersection splits into two sextics.

He distinguishes three kinds of sextic on a quadric: the complete intersection with a cubic surface; the residual intersection with a quartic surface containing two lines of the same ruling; and the residual intersection with a quintic surface containing four lines of that ruling. The original intersection count, including its invoked input, is retained here.
