## Preface

The purpose of this treatise is to give a brief account of the principal properties then known of quartic surfaces with nodes or double curves.

The Kummer surface, including its special forms the tetrahedroid and the wave surface, would naturally have a prominent place. Jessop omits a detailed treatment because R. W. H. T. Hudson’s *Kummer’s Quartic Surface* already provides one. Ruled quartic surfaces are also omitted.

An Introduction summarizes the principal results of the book. Jessop acknowledges H. F. Baker’s encouragement and criticism and thanks the University Press for the printing.

C. M. Jessop, March 1916.

## Introduction: Chapter I

Drawing on Cayley and Rohn, this chapter classifies quartic surfaces by their isolated nodes, assuming that they have no double curves. There can be at most sixteen such nodes. Rohn’s method for more than seven nodes uses seven-nodal plane sextics.

Cayley: “Recent researches,” *Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society*, 1869–71.

Twelve nodes on the surface produce eleven nodes on a plane sextic. The sextic must then be reducible. The successive decompositions end with six lines, corresponding to the sixteen-nodal Kummer surface.
