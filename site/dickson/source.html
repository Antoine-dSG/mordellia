Edition and source

L. E. Dickson, History of the Theory of Numbers, Volume I: Divisibility and Primality. Washington: Carnegie Institution of Washington, 1919. The title and publication leaves identify this edition; later reprint covers and introductions are not used.

The source is the University of California scan on Internet Archive, digitized from a UCLA copy. Stored PDF, archive metadata and scan mapping are retained locally. Retrieval: 14 October 2026. The PDF has 516 pages; source hashes and selected-excerpt hashes are in the v1 manifest.

Edition-specific rights review

This project uses the 1919 volume, not a modern reprint's added material. Dickson is the named author; the Preface acknowledges eleven readers and advisers. Those acknowledgments do not by themselves establish joint authorship, but independently authored contributions would require their own assessment before broader republication.

  • United States: the maximum 95-year term described by the US Copyright Office for protected works of this period has expired for the 1919 publication (no later than the end of 2014).
  • Ordinary UK author-based baseline: Dickson died in 1954, documented by the National Academy memoir. Under the life-plus-70 rule, that ordinary term ended at the end of 2024. This is an author/text baseline, not a conclusion covering independently authored contributions or all special cases.
  • Scope: the initial edition review was conducted for a private loopback preview. Neither online availability nor the archive's rights label is treated as worldwide clearance. Other jurisdictions and wider publication remain unassessed; no modern editorial additions are included.

Dickson's chapter is the immediate historical source for the mathematical reconstructions. References to earlier papers are his bibliographical references unless separately identified. A successful calculation checks mathematics, not attribution or the completeness of Dickson's historical search.
