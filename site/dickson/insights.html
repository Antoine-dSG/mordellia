Dickson Volume I: insight record

Current record, 14 October 2026. The exploratory synthesis preceded the exposition choice. The audience selected for this treatment is a mathematically mature reader with elementary algebra and unique prime factorization. Congruences are introduced locally; logarithms/calculus occur only in the optional Putnam derivation. This audience choice is provisional for this treatment, not a rule for subsequent chapters.

No new theorem, invariant or original discovery is established. The mechanisms below are classical or already explicit in Dickson's account. Classification here means established explanation or verified equivalence within stated hypotheses, not a novelty ranking based on unfamiliarity.

Euclid's divisor list, p. 3 A divisor of a coprime product is a unique product of divisors; σ is multiplicative. The calculation exposes what fails when prime factors overlap. Unique factorization and a geometric sum. Written proof; independent divisor enumeration checks. This is a classical reformulation.
Euler's converse, p. 19; Dickson's converse, p. 30 Two known divisors exhaust a positive sum. Dickson's own proof is both shorter and avoids a coincident-divisor case in the reported longer argument. Only divisibility and positivity. No claim to have invented the short proof. The p. 19 issue is an editorial observation about the displayed argument, not a counterexample to its theorem or a historical-priority claim.
Odd-perfect shape and prime bounds, pp. 19, 24–31 Parity singles out one prime power; σ(n)/n factors into local sums bounded independently of exponents. This separates structural obstructions from a failed finite search. Congruences modulo 4; the optional logarithmic bound adds calculus. Necessary conditions only. General finiteness and the four-prime exclusion remain reported inputs.
Fermat and Descartes, pp. 33–35 Fermat's quotient is 8−9/(2ⁿ+1); Descartes' rules are changes in individual local factors. Both useful outputs and exclusions become transparent. Exact rational arithmetic. All five identities proved and checked. Infinitely many formal transformations do not imply infinitely many admissible numbers.
Thabit and Descartes, pp. 39–40 Equal total divisor sums plus their common value M+N; explicit change of parameter t=2ⁿ⁻¹. Divisor product. Verified equivalence of formulas and conditional proof. The historical attribution remains Dickson's report, not a reconstructed priority claim.
Euler's five problems, pp. 42–44 Match residual divisor sums; impose the abundance of a common factor; complete a product. Euler already supplies the central algebra. Elementary algebra, gcd and prime tests. First ansatz exhaustive at fixed a; other mechanisms explained with explicit hypotheses and selected examples. Paganini shows a limitation of the separated-common-factor ansatz.
Aliquot sequences and generalizations, pp. 48–50 Fixed points, cycles, equal-image fibers, and equal-total tuples are distinct structures attached to s or σ. Equal-entry tuples reconnect to multiplicity. Elementary functions and iteration. Five- and 28-cycles and both triples checked exactly. A cutoff is inconclusive. No general eventual-behavior assertion is inferred.

Editorial conclusions

Dickson's topical organization should be preserved in Original and Modernized. Reinterpreted earns its space chiefly by supplying missing explanations and connecting repeated operations; a new abstract vocabulary alone would add little. Euler's integral-shaped divisor-sum notation becomes σ in Modernized without replacing his factorization route. Original excerpts retain mathematical slips and date-specific assertions, with comments outside the source text.

The opening definitions σ, s, coprimality, exact prime exponents and A are reusable foundations for later chapters. Do not create rival definitions in a later isolated pilot. Keep the user's source-order progression. The treatment covers the main mathematical mechanisms while clearly recording the untranscribed historical catalogue; expansion of that transcription remains distinct from extending the mathematics.

Questions deliberately left open

A wider search for new structure, contemporary status reviews, exhaustive reconstruction of Euler's tables, and the level of expert or reader validation are not settled here. This chapter identifies reusable mechanisms; it does not authorize a new research programme or a jump to a later volume. Possible later investigations would need a specific statement, source comparison and literature check before any novelty claim.
