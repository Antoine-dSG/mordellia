## Amicable numbers

Two positive integers $M,N$ are **amicable** if $s(M)=N$ and $s(N)=M$, where $s$ is the sum of proper positive divisors. Dickson also records *amiable, agreeable, befreundete,* and *verwandte*.

## Thabit’s construction

Dickson reports that Thabit ben Korrah<sup>10</sup> gives the amicable pair $2^nht,2^nr$ when

$$h=3\cdot2^n-1,\qquad t=3\cdot2^{n-1}-1,\qquad r=9\cdot2^{2n-1}-1$$

are all primes greater than 2. In the literal formulation, put

$$z=1+2+\cdots+2^n,\quad h=z+2^n,\quad t=z-2^{n-1},\quad r=(2^{n+1}+2^{n-2})2^{n+1}-1.$$

The Latin term is *se invicem amantes*. In his French translation of this Arabic manuscript, Woepcke<sup>10</sup> notes a definition under the name *congeneres* in the arithmetic treatise (number 51) of Ikhovân Alçafâ, manuscript 1105, anciens fonds arabes, p. 15, National Library of Paris.

## Descartes’ formulation

Descartes<sup>355</sup> takes a power of 2, $t\ge2$, such that $3t-1$, $6t-1$ and $18t^2-1$ are prime. Then $2t(18t^2-1)$ is one member of an amicable pair. The initial powers $2,8,64$ give $284,18416,9437056$; their proper-divisor sums give the other members, starting with 220. The third pair is

$$9363584=2^7\cdot191\cdot383,\qquad9437056=2^7\cdot73727.$$

Dickson identifies this with formula (1) on taking $t=2^{n-1}$. Descartes<sup>356</sup> says that Fermat's rule agrees exactly with his own.
