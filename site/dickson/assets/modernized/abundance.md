## A bound on the least prime factor

Putnam<sup>182</sup> proves that if a perfect number has $r$ distinct prime factors, at least one is at most $1+r/\log 2$. Consequently they cannot all be at least $1+3r/2$.

<div class="historical-note"><sup>182</sup> Amer. Math. Monthly, 17, 1910, 167.</div>

## Dickson’s converse

Dickson<sup>183</sup> gives a short proof of the classification of even perfect numbers. Suppose $2^a m$ is perfect, where $m$ is odd and $a>0$. Let $\sigma(m)$ be the sum of all positive divisors of $m$. Then

$$(2^{a+1}-1)\sigma(m)=2^{a+1}m.$$

Thus $\sigma(m)=m+d$, where $d=m/(2^{a+1}-1)$. The integer $d$ divides $m$. Since the sum of all divisors is already $m+d$, these are its only divisors. Therefore $d=1$ and $m$ is prime.

<div class="historical-note"><sup>183</sup> Ibid., 18, 1911, 109.</div>

## Primitive non-deficient numbers

Dickson<sup>193</sup> calls a number **non-deficient** when $\sigma(n)\ge2n$, and **primitive** when none of its proper divisors is non-deficient. He proves that fixing the number of distinct odd prime factors and the exponent of 2 leaves only finitely many primitive non-deficient numbers. The corollary is that there are only finitely many odd perfect numbers with a fixed number of distinct prime factors.

He reports that an odd abundant number has at least three distinct prime factors, and gives the following primitive ones with exactly three:

$$3^3\cdot5\cdot7,\quad3^2\cdot5^2\cdot7,\quad3^2\cdot5\cdot7^2,\quad3^3\cdot5^2\cdot11,$$
$$3^5\cdot5^2\cdot13,\quad3^4\cdot5^3\cdot13,\quad3^4\cdot5^2\cdot13^2,\quad3^3\cdot5^3\cdot13^2.$$

His paper lists many primitive odd abundant numbers with four distinct prime factors, and even non-deficient numbers of specified types. It determines all primitive non-deficient numbers below 15000: 23 odd and 78 even. Dickson concludes from these lists that no odd perfect number has four or fewer distinct prime factors, and compares Sylvester.<sup>148–153</sup>

<div class="historical-note"><sup>193</sup> Amer. Jour. Math., 35, 1913, 413–26.</div>
