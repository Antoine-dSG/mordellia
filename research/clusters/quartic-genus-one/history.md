# History

Mordell uses quartics in several ways. Equations of the form
\[
    y^2=ax^4+bx^3+cx^2+dx+e
\]
are genus-one curves when the quartic has no repeated root. Mordell records their birational relationship with cubic Weierstrass models and the associated binary quartic invariants.

The Pell-type equations
\[
    x^4-Dy^4=\pm1,\qquad y^2=Dx^4\pm1
\]
belong to the Ljunggren--Nagell--Mordell tradition: solutions are constrained by fundamental units in quadratic fields, and in many cases there is at most one or at most two positive solutions.

Modern arithmetic treats binary quartic models as genus-one curves and as 2-coverings of elliptic curves. Bhargava--Shankar count binary quartic forms with bounded invariants and use the resulting orbit counts to control the average size of 2-Selmer groups and average ranks of elliptic curves. Bhargava--Cremona--Fisher study local solubility densities for genus-one curves given by binary quartics.

Conclusion: this is a strong deep-dive cluster because Mordell's quartic entries sit at the intersection of classical Pell/unit methods, Thue finiteness, binary quartic invariant theory, and modern Selmer/torsor geometry.
