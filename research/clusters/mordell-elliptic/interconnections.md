# Interconnections

`LEC-060` is the general structural theorem: rational points on a nonsingular genus-one cubic form a finitely generated group.

`LEC-066` is the \(j=0\) one-parameter subfamily. The equations `LEC-084`--`LEC-092` are special values of \(k\), with Mordell using them as concrete tests for the same mechanisms:

- `LEC-084`, `LEC-085`: classical integral-solution cases.
- `LEC-086`: exceptional rational finite case \(k=1\).
- `LEC-087`: no rational affine solutions for \(k=7\).
- `LEC-088`: exactly eight integer solutions for \(k=17\).
- `LEC-089`--`LEC-092`: no integer solutions for \(k=34,45,58,70\).

`LEC-190` is the analytic continuation of the same story: once Mordell-Weil finite generation produces a rank, Birch--Swinnerton-Dyer predicts that rank from the \(L\)-function.

Adjacent Mordell entries `LEC-068`--`LEC-083` should eventually be folded into this cluster. They are not structurally separate; they are further specializations of \(y^2=x^3+k\) or related congruence/descent cases. `LEC-067` is the local congruence shadow of the same family.

The most useful graph for this cluster should not merely connect equations by visual similarity. It should connect them by obstruction/method:

- group law and finite generation;
- \(j=0\) cubic twist;
- \(3\)-descent and class groups;
- binary cubic forms and Thue equations;
- integral-point algorithms;
- analytic rank and BSD.

This gives a reusable template for later genus-one equations: identify the elliptic model, decide whether the problem is about rational points or integral points, then choose the relevant layer of the graph.
