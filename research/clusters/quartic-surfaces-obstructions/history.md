# History

`LEC-119` is an explicit impossibility theorem. Mordell imposes congruence hypotheses on the coefficients of quadratic forms
\[
    F=ax^2+by^2+cz^2,\quad
    F_1=a_1x^2+b_1y^2+c_1z^2,\quad
    G=px^2+qy^2+rz^2
\]
and proves that
\[
    k_1FF_1=k_2G^2
\]
has no nonzero integer solutions. The natural modern setting is a quartic surface with a local obstruction, especially at \(2\).

`LEC-122` is a diagonal quartic surface. Mordell's statement is constructive: from a known rational point with nonzero coordinates and square coefficient product, one obtains another rational point unless the corresponding line lies on the surface. Modern work on diagonal quartic surfaces studies existence, search, density, and the role of the 48 lines.

History ranks:

- `LEC-119`: rank 2. It is an instance of local solubility and obstruction theory for quartic surfaces and intersections built from quadratic forms.
- `LEC-122`: rank 2. It is an instance of the diagonal quartic surface problem, with substantial later literature on rational points and density.
