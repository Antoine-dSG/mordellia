# History

## Mordell

`LEC-096` occurs in Mordell, pp. 106--110, as
\[
    x^2+y^2+z^2-axyz=b.
\]
Mordell explicitly identifies the case \(a=3,b=0\) as classical Markoff, recalls the Markoff chain, and states that non-trivial solutions are generated from fundamental solutions by elementary operations. He also relates the case \(b<0\) to Hurwitz's higher-dimensional equation.

`LEC-117`, `LEC-118`, and `LEC-183` occur in Mordell, pp. 292--300. Mordell first considers
\[
    g(x)+h(y)+c=zxy,
\]
with \(g,h\) without constant term, and reduces it to congruences modulo \(xy\). The special cases
\[
    x^2+y^2+1=xyz,\qquad x^2+y^2\pm x\pm y+1=xyz
\]
are treated by divisibility conditions and recurrence sequences.

## Literature Placement

`LEC-096` is a special case of the Markoff-Hurwitz family. Modern work studies automorphism groups, strong approximation, integral points, and asymptotic counting for Markoff-type surfaces and higher-dimensional Markoff-Hurwitz varieties.

Relevant references:

- Markoff's original equation and Markoff chains: A. A. Markoff, "Sur les formes quadratiques binaires indefinies", Math. Ann. 15 (1879), 381--406; Math. Ann. 17 (1880), 379--399.
- Mordell's cited source for `LEC-096`: Hurwitz's study of the higher-dimensional equation, as referenced by Mordell on p. 107.
- Bourgain, Gamburd, and Sarnak study Vieta-generated morphism groups on Markoff and related affine cubic surfaces, with strong approximation applications: https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.06411.
- Hu, Tan, and Zhang determine polynomial automorphisms preserving the Markoff-Hurwitz polynomial \(x_1^2+\cdots+x_n^2-x_1\cdots x_n\): https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.06955.
- Chen studies twisted Markoff surfaces \(ax^2+y^2+z^2-xyz=m\) and Brauer-Manin obstructions: https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06864.

`LEC-117` and `LEC-118` are special cases of the Barnes/Mordell family
\[
    x^2+y^2+c=xyz
\]
and its shifted variants. Mordell cites E. S. Barnes, "On the diophantine equation \(x^2+y^2+c=xyz\)", J. London Math. Soc.

`LEC-183` is best viewed as a general exchange relation. Its modern analogue is not ordinary cluster algebra alone, because ordinary cluster exchange polynomials are binomial. The closer framework is Laurent phenomenon algebras, which allow more general exchange polynomials.

Relevant references:

- Fomin and Zelevinsky introduce the Laurent phenomenon for birational recurrences: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0104241.
- Lam and Pylyavskyy define Laurent phenomenon algebras, allowing arbitrary irreducible exchange polynomials rather than only binomials: https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.2611.
- Hikami relates cluster mutations to automorphisms of character varieties for the once-punctured torus and four-punctured sphere: https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.03379.

## Provisional History Ranks

- `LEC-096`: history rank 1. It is a special case of a broader Markoff-Hurwitz/Markoff-surface theory.
- `LEC-117`: history rank 2. It is an instance of the Barnes/Mordell family, with related instances treated in the literature.
- `LEC-118`: history rank 2. Same family as `LEC-117`, with shifts.
- `LEC-183`: history rank 2. It is an instance of the broader exchange/Laurent-phenomenon pattern, but the full Mordell polynomial family is broader than the most standard cluster-algebra setting.
