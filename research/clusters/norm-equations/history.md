# History

Mordell treats \(x^2+y^2=z^n\) through unique factorization in the Gaussian integers:
\[
    x+iy=i^r(a+ib)^n,\qquad z=a^2+b^2.
\]
This is the prototype: a Diophantine equation becomes a statement about factorization in a ring of integers.

The higher-degree entries `LEC-179` and `LEC-180` use norm forms in algebraic number fields. Mordell states finiteness theorems under hypotheses involving the degree of the field, the rank of the unit group, and linear independence of the coefficients.

Modern norm-form theory is tied to the Subspace Theorem, unit equations, and height bounds for representatives of solution classes. Akhtari--Vaaler study height bounds for norm-form equations and emphasize the natural decomposition of the solution set into equivalence classes. Bajpai's recent work records that effective resolution of norm-form equations in more than two variables is substantially harder than for Thue equations, while extending effective methods to new classes.

Conclusion: this is a rank-1 history target for `LEC-178` and a rank-2 history target for the higher-degree norm-form entries. The overall cluster is structurally strong because it connects elementary parametrisation, unit groups, and modern subspace-theorem finiteness.
