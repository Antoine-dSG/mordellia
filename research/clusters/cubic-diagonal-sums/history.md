# History

Mordell treats the cubic-sum equations in several distinct modes.

For `LEC-041`, the equation \(x^3+y^3=az^3\) is a diagonal cubic curve. Mordell records non-solubility results over \(\mathbf Q(\rho)\), where \(\rho^2+\rho+1=0\), and rational non-solubility for specific prime-supported values of \(a\). In modern terms these are \(j=0\) cubic twists, closely related to elliptic curves \(E_k:x^3+y^3=k\) and to explicit 3-isogeny descent.

For `LEC-043`, the equation
\[
    ax^3+by^3+cz^3+dxyz=0
\]
contains the Hesse pencil as the case \(a=b=c=1\). Mordell's dichotomies between finitely many exceptional points and infinitely many points are elliptic-curve statements in plane-cubic form.

For `LEC-100`, the sum of three cubes has the elementary obstruction \(n\equiv \pm4\pmod 9\). Beyond that obstruction, the general integer problem is open. Modern computation has solved many individual \(n\), including the formerly difficult cases \(33\) and \(42\), but there is no complete structural criterion.

For `LEC-108` and `LEC-109`, Mordell records or cites complete solutions for the equations \(z^2=x^3+y^3\) and \(2z^2=x^3+y^3\). These are low-complexity Fermat-type cubic equations with cyclotomic factorization and parametrisation.

For `LEC-114`, Mordell records the four-cubes conjecture and the known result for \(n\not\equiv\pm4\pmod9\). This is part of the classical four-cubes problem: the remaining obstruction class is \(n\equiv\pm4\pmod9\).

History ranks:

- `LEC-041`: rank 2. It is an instance of the general \(j=0\) elliptic-curve/cubic-twist problem, with many solved subfamilies.
- `LEC-043`: rank 2. It is an instance of the plane-cubic/Hesse-pencil theory, with the general elliptic-curve structure known.
- `LEC-100`: rank 2. It is the central sum-of-three-cubes problem; many instances are solved, but the general criterion is open.
- `LEC-108`: rank 1. Mordell's equation is a solved special Fermat-type curve with explicit parametrisation.
- `LEC-109`: rank 2. Mordell defers the complete solution; structurally it is the same Fermat-type family as `LEC-108`.
- `LEC-114`: rank 2. It is an active instance of the four-cubes problem with large solved congruence classes.
