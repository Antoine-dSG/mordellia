# Claim

## Precise Claim

For a positive integer \(d\), the equation
\[
    xy+yz+zx=d,
    \qquad x,y,z>0,
\]
has no solution if and only if either
\[
    d\in\{1,4,18\},
\]
or \(d\equiv2\pmod4\) is squarefree and the ideal class group of
\(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-d})\), whose discriminant is \(-4d\), has exponent at most
\(2\). Equivalently, the latter \(d\) are squarefree idoneal numbers in that
congruence class.

There is also a symmetry-sensitive form: \(d\) is idoneal if and only if
\[
    xy+yz+zx=d
\]
has no solution in distinct positive integers \(0<x<y<z\).

## Mordell Atom

- Atom id: `ATOM-LEC-026-POSITIVE-NONREPRESENTATION`.
- Type: strict-positive representation obstruction.
- LEC tag: `LEC-026`.
- Mordell pages: pp. 291--292.

## Modern Object

Idoneal discriminants and elementary \(2\)-class groups of imaginary
quadratic orders.

## Qualitative Behavior Explained

The absence of positive solutions is determined by a global class-group
condition, not by an isolated congruence obstruction.  The possible
exceptional values of \(d\) are therefore tied to the idoneal-number problem
and to the classification of imaginary quadratic class groups of exponent
\(2\).
