# Claim

## Precise Claim

The cubic divisibility specialization of `LEC-183`,
\[
    xy\mid ax^3+by^3+c,
\]
is the integral-point problem on the affine cubic surface
\[
    S:\quad xyz=ax^3+by^3+c.
\]
More generally, consider
\[
 S_{A,B}:\quad
 xyz=ax^3+by^3+c+a_2x^2+a_1x+b_2y^2+b_1y,
 \qquad ab\neq0.
\]
Kollar and Villalobos-Paz construct Geiser-type isomorphisms between companion
surfaces; their composites give infinite-order integral automorphisms.  In the
cubic case the resulting infinite cyclic group has finite index in the
automorphism group under their stated nondegeneracy hypotheses.

Kollar and Li use the companion-surface groupoid and its integral orbits to
prove that every surface \(S_{A,B}\) above has infinitely many integral points.
Mordell's recursive congruence chain is therefore an arithmetic shadow of an
infinite discrete automorphism group of an affine cubic surface.

## Mordell Atom

- Atom ids: `ATOM-LEC-183-EXCHANGE-CONGRUENCE`,
  `ATOM-LEC-183-CUBIC-DIVISIBILITY-CHAIN`.
- Type: variable-modulus congruence / recursive generation of solutions.
- LEC tag: `LEC-183`.
- Mordell pages: pp. 293--295.

## Modern Object

Affine cubic surfaces with nodal boundary and an \(A_2\) singularity at
infinity, Geiser-type involutions, and an infinite companion-surface
automorphism groupoid.

## Qualitative Behavior Explained

The infinite-order automorphism organizes integral points into arithmetic
orbits and explains why one seed solution yields infinitely many.  This gives
a geometric source for Mordell's recurrence that does not rely on a cluster
algebra interpretation.
