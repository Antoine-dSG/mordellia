# Literature

## Modern Formulation

Schinzel completed and corrected assertions in Mordell's treatment of
\[
    f(x)+g(y)+c\equiv0\pmod{xy}.
\]

Kollar and Villalobos-Paz study affine surfaces \(xyz=g(x,y)\). Their
projective closure has an irreducible nodal cubic at infinity and an
\(A_2\)-singularity at the node. They construct Geiser-type involutions and
companion-surface isomorphisms whose composite is an infinite-order
automorphism.  For cubic polynomials they describe the automorphism group up
to finite index.

Kollar and Li apply these isomorphisms arithmetically.  For every
\(a,b\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\) and
\(a_1,a_2,b_1,b_2,c\in\mathbb Z\), they prove that
\[
    xyz=ax^3+by^3+c+a_2x^2+a_1x+b_2y^2+b_1y
\]
has infinitely many integral solutions.  Their proof finds a point with an
infinite orbit under the companion-surface groupoid.

## Relation To Mordell

- divisibility by \(xy\) = integrality of the third surface coordinate;
- recursive solution chain = orbit under companion-surface isomorphisms;
- return after a cycle = infinite-order automorphism of one cubic surface;
- growth in Mordell's sequence = escape of an automorphism orbit;
- infinitely many solutions = existence of an infinite integral orbit.
