# Claim

## Precise Claim

The Erdos--Straus equation
\[
    \frac4n=\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z
\]
is the positive integral-point problem on
\[
    U_n:\quad 4xyz=n(xy+xz+yz).
\]
Its projective closure
\[
    S_n:\quad
    4X_1X_2X_3
    =nX_0(X_1X_2+X_1X_3+X_2X_3)
\]
is isomorphic over \(\mathbb Q\) to Cayley's four-nodal cubic surface.  Indeed,
putting \(Y_0=-nX_0/4\) gives
\[
    X_1X_2X_3+Y_0X_1X_2+Y_0X_1X_3+Y_0X_2X_3=0.
\]

For prime \(n=p\), a solution gives the primitive Cayley-cubic point
\[
    (-p,4x,4y,4z),
    \qquad
    \frac1{-p}+\frac1{4x}+\frac1{4y}+\frac1{4z}=0.
\]
Mordell's two cases, according as one or two of \(x,y,z\) are divisible by
\(p\), are the Type I and Type II charts in the modern classification of
primitive points on this surface.  The coprime factor coordinates used in
that classification describe charts on the universal torsor of Cayley's
cubic.

## Modern Object

Cayley's four-nodal cubic surface and its universal torsor.

## Qualitative Behavior Explained

The torsor accounts for the completeness of Mordell's two prime-denominator
parametrisations and for their multiplicative, divisor-rich form.  It also
places solution counting in the geometry of primitive integral points on
Cayley's cubic.  It does not prove that every fibre parameter \(n\) has a
positive integral point.
