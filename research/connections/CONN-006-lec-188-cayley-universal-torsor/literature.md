# Literature

## Modern Formulation

Elsholtz and Tao regard the Erdos--Straus equation as the affine surface
\[
    S_n=\{(x,y,z):4xyz=n(yz+xz+xy)\}.
\]
Their Propositions 2.2 and 2.6 classify Type I and Type II solutions by
six coprime-factor coordinates.  In Remark 2.10 they state that these are
special cases of Heath-Brown's classification of primitive integral points on
Cayley's surface
\[
    X_0^{-1}+X_1^{-1}+X_2^{-1}+X_3^{-1}=0,
\]
and that the factor coordinates essentially describe the closure of its
universal torsor.  They then identify Mordell's two standard cases explicitly.

Heath-Brown uses the universal-torsor factorisation to prove that the number of
non-trivial primitive points of height at most \(B\) on Cayley's cubic has
order \(B(\log B)^6\), as predicted by Manin's conjecture.

## Relation To Mordell

- Mordell's prime denominator \(p\) = the distinguished Cayley coordinate
  \(X_0=-p\).
- One denominator divisible by \(p\) = Type I torsor chart.
- Two denominators divisible by \(p\) = Type II torsor chart.
- Mordell's pairwise coprime \(a,b,c\) and common factor \(d\) =
  universal-torsor coprimality and factor coordinates.
- Mordell's many arithmetic-progressions of solvable \(n\) = integral
  specialisations of low-dimensional torsor charts.
